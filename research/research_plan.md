# LeaveBy research plan

Documented October 20, 2026 as a retrospective account of the research approach visible in the existing files. The interview records are dated September 19. This document does not claim a plan was saved before those interviews.

## Research question

How do people manage the transition from a workout to a fixed class or work commitment, and which conditions make that transition feel rushed?

## Starting assumption

The [initial problem statement](problem_statement.md) identifies a short gym-to-class window with little buffer. The [What / How / Why exercise](what_how_why.md) asks whether others share the pressure and whether commute distance or preparation routines change it.

## Method and sample

The repository contains three interview records: [Chris](../interviews/Chris_2026-09-19.md), [David](../interviews/David_2026-09-19.md), and [Selina](../interviews/Selina_2026-09-19.md). Each record labels the participant as outside the immediate friend group. Their contexts cover a longer train commute, a short walk to class, and a gym-to-work transition.

The [interview script](interview_questions.md) focuses on past events: the latest transition, a difficult example, a smoother comparison, existing workarounds and effects on the rest of the day. Interview duration, recording method and recruitment details beyond the recorded relationship/context are not documented.

## Analysis

1. Compare answers within each interview.
2. Group observations across interviews in the [affinity map](affinity_map.md).
3. Check patterns against contrasting evidence, especially David's usually manageable routine.
4. Form [insight statements](insight_statements.md), a [behavioral persona](persona.md), and a [point of view](point_of_view.md).
5. Use these findings to explain the scope and design, then test the prototype separately.

The original [Class 4 synthesis](synthesis.md) predates the October 20 organization of the separate affinity, insight, persona and POV files.

## Limits

Three interview accounts can reveal useful differences, but they cannot establish prevalence, a universally sufficient buffer, or whether LeaveBy reduces lateness. Participant answers are self-reports. No direct observation or field trial is documented.

## Prototype evaluation

The pending peer session will ask another person to create a plan, explain the two action times, and change the commute. Record completion, hesitation, help needed and feedback using the [peer feedback sheet](../docs/peer-feedback.md). Do not count calculation tests as usability evidence.
