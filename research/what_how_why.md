# Class 2 — What / How / Why

## What / How / Why

| # | WHAT — observable facts only | HOW — pace, effort, or mood | WHY — my best explanation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | I finish the gym around 8:30 AM, then shower, get ready, and travel to school for a 10:00 AM class. | Rushed | Several required steps have to fit inside a short fixed window. A delay at the gym, while getting ready, or during the commute reduces the small amount of buffer time before class. |
| 2 | My class ends at either 3:00 PM or 5:00 PM depending on the day, and I go directly to work until around 8:00 or 9:00 PM. | Tired | There is little recovery time between school and work, so one responsibility immediately turns into the next without much time to rest, eat, or reset. |
| 3 | After spending most of the day at school and work, I commute home around 8:00 or 9:00 PM. | Exhausted | The commute happens after a long chain of activities that began early in the morning, so physical and mental fatigue has accumulated by the time I leave work. |

## Q&A

**What pattern do I notice across the three moments?**  
The individual activities are manageable on their own. The strongest friction appears during the transitions between activities when there is little or no buffer time.

**Which moment feels most useful to investigate further?**  
The gym-to-school transition is the strongest candidate because it repeats, has a fixed deadline, is directly observable, and is narrow enough to investigate without trying to solve my entire daily schedule.

**What am I still unsure about?**  
I need to find out whether other people experience the same pressure, which parts of the transition create the most friction for them, and whether the problem changes when commute distance or preparation routines are different.
