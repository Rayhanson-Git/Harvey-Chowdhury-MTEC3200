# Point of view and How Might We

Documented October 20, 2026 from the existing interview evidence and synthesis.

## Point of view

A student or working commuter who exercises before a fixed commitment needs a clear boundary between workout time and transition time because unpredictable waits can consume a small buffer, leading them to shorten the routine or rush to arrive.

## Evidence

- **User and situation:** Chris moves from gym to class, Selina from gym to work, and David from gym to class.
- **Need:** Chris already uses a hard stop and Selina uses a warning alarm. All three describe cutting or stopping exercise when behind.
- **Insight:** equipment, shower and transit delays can reduce available transition time. David's predictable short walk and larger buffer provide a contrasting, usually manageable case.

See [affinity cards C1–C6, D1–D6 and S1–S8](affinity_map.md) and [insights 1–4](insight_statements.md).

## How Might We

How might we help someone manage the transition between a workout and a fixed commitment without small unexpected delays turning the whole transition into a rush?

This question is retained from the [Class 4 synthesis](synthesis.md#how-might-we).

## Design response and testable assumption

The selected response is a planner that works backward from the commitment and shows a workout-stop time separately from departure. Preparation, commute and buffer remain explicit estimates.

The pending peer test must establish whether people understand these two action times and can edit a plan. The interviews do not establish that the app prevents lateness or reduces stress.
