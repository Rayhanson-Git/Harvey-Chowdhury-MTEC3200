# Class 4 — Synthesis & Brainstorming

## Interview Synthesis

 The general trend I saw throughout the three interviews is that after the exercise component, they start to become stressed when they have a set exercise then a set time to make up for it but no padding for late starts. Both Chris and Selina shared examples of when their workout had to be shortened, something cancelled out or they had to constantly look at the clock. But, David's routine was somewhat the same except he didn't get rushed as often because his commute was shorter and more predictable.

This is how I viewed my initial problem. It's not so much the exercise as the workout. The larger problem is that there are a number of things that must occur between the workout and next commitment, and some of those things are beyond our control. Time to wait for equipment, shower, get ready and commute can all be longer than usual.

I was most surprised at David's experience. He takes pretty much the same path but with his commute being short, predictable, and he has more spare time, the transition is much easier for him. It was there that I learned that buffer and the predictability of the transition is important rather than working out before school or work.

The actual issue is that there can be short, unpredictable gaps between a workout and a concrete time commitment, which forces people to rush, reduce their workout, leave part of it or risk being late.

## Real Problem

Small unpredictable delays can use up the limited buffer between a workout and a fixed commitment, forcing someone to rush, cut parts of their routine, or risk being late.

## How Might We

How might we help someone manage the transition between a workout and a fixed commitment without small unexpected delays turning the whole transition into a rush?

## Brainstorming

- A countdown that shows how much time is left before I need to leave the gym.
- A hard-stop reminder that tells me when I should end my workout.
- A planner that works backward from the start time of class or work.
- A tool that adds extra buffer time for getting ready and commuting.
- A simple checklist for everything that needs to happen between the gym and the next commitment.
- A warning system that changes depending on how much buffer time is left.
- A physical reminder inside a gym bag showing the leave-by time.
- A smartwatch or wearable vibration reminder for when it is time to stop working out.
- A system that shortens the remaining workout if I start running behind.
- A quick way to compare the planned leave time with the actual leave time.
- A routine that divides the transition into workout, shower, changing, food, and commute time.
- A green, yellow, and red visual system showing how much buffer is left.
- A tool that adjusts the leave-by time when the commute is delayed.
- A one-tap "running late" mode that immediately shows the new leave-by time.
- A system that remembers which parts of the transition usually take longer and adds extra buffer for them.

## Idea Selection

### Sensible One

A planner that works backward from the start time of class or work and gives the user a clear hard-stop time for the workout.

### Most Likely to Delight

A simple green, yellow, and red visual system that shows how much buffer time is left before the user needs to leave.

### Favorite

A transition planner that combines the next commitment time, preparation time, commute time, and extra buffer to show exactly when the user should stop the workout and leave.

### Long Shot

A system that automatically reacts to delays in the workout or commute and adjusts the remaining workout time and leave-by time in real time.

## Chosen Direction

A lightweight transition planner that works backward from a fixed class or work start time and gives the user a clear hard-stop time for their workout.

It helps the user protect enough time for getting ready and commuting so that small delays are less likely to turn the entire transition into a rush.
