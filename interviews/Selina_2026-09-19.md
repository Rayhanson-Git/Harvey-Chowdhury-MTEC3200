# Interview — Selina

**Participant:** Selina  
**Date:** September 19, 2026  
**Relationship / context:** We go to the same gym; student who also works  
**Outside immediate friend group:** Yes  
**Relevant routine:** Gym → shower/change → work

## Answers

**Q: Walk me through a normal day when you work out before work.**  
I normally go late morning or early afternoon because I work later. I spend about an hour at the gym, shower there, get dressed, and go directly to work.

**Q: Does that transition usually feel rushed?**  
Sometimes. My work schedule is fixed, so once I get close to the time I need to leave, I start checking the clock constantly.

**Q: What causes delays?**  
Usually the gym being crowded. If I have to wait for equipment, everything gets pushed back.

**Q: What happens when you realize you are behind schedule?**  
I start cutting things. Maybe I do fewer sets or skip an exercise.

**Q: Which part takes the most time after the workout?**  
Honestly, showering and getting ready. The workout time is predictable. Getting ready can randomly take ten minutes longer depending on how busy it is.

**Q: Do you prepare anything in advance?**  
I keep a separate gym bag that already has most of the things I need. That helps a lot.

**Q: Do you think the problem is that you do not have enough free time overall?**  
Not really. I might have time later at night. It is more that the specific window between the gym and work is tight.

**Q: Have you found anything that helps?**  
I started setting an alarm about thirty minutes before I need to leave. That is basically my warning to stop starting new exercises.

**Q: What makes a smooth day different?**  
When I start the workout on time. If I start twenty minutes late, I feel behind the entire time.

**Q: Is there anything else important about the routine?**  
I think a lot of it is mental too. Even if I technically have enough time, knowing I have somewhere to be makes the workout feel rushed.

## Synthesis

**Strongest observations:**  
- Selina’s total free time is not the main issue. The friction comes from the fixed work deadline immediately after the gym.
- A crowded gym can push the entire schedule back because waiting for equipment delays the workout.
- Showering and getting ready are less predictable than the workout itself and can add around ten minutes.
- When she falls behind, she cuts sets or skips an exercise.
- She uses a packed gym bag and a thirty-minute warning alarm to reduce the pressure.

**Evidence or wording worth keeping:**  
- Selina described the problem as the specific window between the gym and work being tight.
- She also said that knowing she has somewhere to be can make the workout feel rushed even when she technically has enough time.

**What supports the current problem statement:**  
- Her experience supports the idea that a fixed commitment after exercise creates pressure when the transition window is tight.
- It also supports the idea that unpredictable delays during the workout or while getting ready can remove the available buffer.

**What challenges or changes the current problem statement:**  
- The problem is not necessarily a lack of free time across the whole day.
- Mental pressure from the upcoming fixed commitment can be part of the friction even before an actual delay happens.

**New question or follow-up raised by this interview:**  
- How much of the stress comes from actual time loss versus the feeling of watching a fixed deadline get closer?
