# Interview — David

**Participant:** David  
**Date:** September 19, 2026  
**Relationship / context:** Classmate  
**Outside immediate friend group:** Yes  
**Relevant routine:** Gym → shower/change → short walk to class

## Answers

**Q: Walk me through a normal day when you work out before class.**  
I usually get to the gym around 8:00. I work out for about an hour, shower there, and then walk to campus. The gym is only about ten minutes away.

**Q: Does that transition feel rushed?**  
Not usually. I think location makes a huge difference.

**Q: When does it become stressful?**  
If I start late or the locker room is crowded. Sometimes every shower is being used and then I am just standing there waiting.

**Q: Which part creates the most friction?**  
Probably the locker room. The walk to school is predictable, so I do not really worry about that.

**Q: What happens if your workout runs late?**  
I stop the workout. I would rather miss the last exercise than walk into class late.

**Q: Do you prepare anything in advance?**  
Yeah, I bring my school clothes, laptop, and everything with me.

**Q: How much buffer time do you have?**  
Usually around twenty or thirty minutes.

**Q: Have you found any routine that makes things easier?**  
I try to use the same locker area and keep everything packed in the same order so I am not searching through my bag.

**Q: Is your biggest problem lack of time?**  
Not really. I think I have enough time most days. It is more about unexpected things slowing me down.

**Q: What makes the transition go smoothly?**  
Starting on time and not having to wait for anything.

**Q: Is there anything you think people with longer commutes experience differently?**  
Yeah. If I had another forty-five minutes of travel after the gym, I would probably feel much more rushed.

## Synthesis

**Strongest observations:**  
- David usually does not feel rushed because the gym is only about a ten-minute walk from campus and he normally has twenty to thirty minutes of buffer.
- The locker room is the main source of friction because shower availability can create an unexpected wait.
- When he runs late, he protects the class deadline by ending the workout rather than risking being late.
- He prepares by bringing his school clothes and laptop and keeping his bag organized in a consistent way.

**Evidence or wording worth keeping:**  
- David said location makes a huge difference.
- He described unexpected slowdowns, rather than a general lack of time, as the main problem.

**What supports the current problem statement:**  
- His experience supports the idea that unpredictable delays can create pressure during the transition.
- Locker-room availability is another example of a small delay that can reduce the available buffer.

**What challenges or changes the current problem statement:**  
- The transition is not automatically stressful for everyone who works out before class.
- A short and predictable commute plus a larger buffer can make the same routine manageable.
- Commute distance and predictability appear to be important conditions that affect how severe the problem is.

**New question or follow-up raised by this interview:**  
- What amount of buffer is enough to make the transition feel manageable when the commute itself is predictable?
