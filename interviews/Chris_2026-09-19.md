# Interview — Chris

**Participant:** Chris  
**Date:** September 19, 2026  
**Relationship / context:** College student, works part-time, and commutes about 45–60 minutes  
**Outside immediate friend group:** Yes  
**Relevant routine:** Works out before morning classes

## Answers

**Q: Walk me through a normal day when you work out before school.**  
I usually wake up around 6:00, get to the gym around 7:00, and try to finish by 8:15 or 8:30. After that I have to shower, change, grab something to eat, and take the train to school. My first class is normally around 10.

**Q: What usually happens between leaving the gym and getting to class?**  
The shower and changing part is usually fine unless the locker room is busy. The bigger issue is the train. I can plan everything perfectly and then lose fifteen minutes waiting.

**Q: Tell me about the last time that transition felt rushed.**  
A few days ago I stayed at the gym longer because I was waiting for a machine. I left maybe fifteen minutes later than usual. Then the train was delayed too. I ended up skipping breakfast and basically running from the station to class.

**Q: Which part of the transition creates the most pressure?**  
Probably the commute because I have the least control over it.

**Q: What do you change when you are running late?**  
Usually I cut my workout short. Sometimes I skip stretching or one of the last exercises. If I am really late, I just grab coffee instead of food.

**Q: Do you prepare anything in advance?**  
Yeah. I pack my school clothes and everything the night before. If I had to go home after the gym, there is no way I would make it to class on time.

**Q: How much buffer time do you normally have?**  
Maybe fifteen or twenty minutes on a good day. It sounds like enough until something small goes wrong.

**Q: Have you created any workaround?**  
I give myself a hard stop time at the gym now. Even if I did not finish everything, I leave.

**Q: What makes the transition go smoothly?**  
Finishing the workout on time, already having my bag packed, and the train actually showing up when it is supposed to.

## Synthesis

**Strongest observations:**  
- Chris has only about fifteen to twenty minutes of buffer on a good day, so a small delay can affect the whole transition.
- Gym delays and transit delays can stack together. In the example he described, waiting for equipment made him leave late, then a train delay caused him to skip breakfast and run to class.
- The commute creates the most pressure because it is the part he has the least control over.
- He already uses two workarounds: packing school clothes the night before and giving himself a hard stop time at the gym.

**Evidence or wording worth keeping:**  
- Chris said the shower and changing part is usually manageable, while the train is the bigger issue.
- He described the buffer as enough until “something small goes wrong.”

**What supports the current problem statement:**  
- His experience supports the idea that a workout-to-class transition becomes stressful when several required steps have to fit inside a short window with little buffer.
- His experience also supports the idea that one small delay can create pressure across the rest of the transition.

**What challenges or changes the current problem statement:**  
- For Chris, the commute is a stronger source of friction than showering or getting ready.
- The problem may be less about the total number of steps and more about how little control the person has over unpredictable delays.
